## 2. Objective and Scope

### 2.1 Objective

This document demonstrates how a wellhead installation activity can be organized into a structured technical procedure covering preparation, equipment inspection, installation planning, connection verification, testing documentation, troubleshooting, and handover.

### 2.2 Scope

The sample addresses documentation for an API 10,000 psi-rated multi-bowl wellhead system intended to accommodate multiple casing strings within a compact wellhead arrangement, subject to the specific manufacturer's design.

The actual equipment configuration, pressure ratings, casing program, connection type, installation sequence, and testing requirements must be confirmed before an operational procedure is issued.

This document is intended for:

## 3. HSE Requirements and Hazards

> **Critical safety notice:** This sample does not authorize field work. All work must be performed under the applicable approved work procedures, risk assessments, Permit to Work (PTW) requirements, and site-specific HSE controls. Pressure isolation, verification of well status, lifting operations, and any hot work must be managed by competent and authorized personnel.

### 3.1 Key Hazards

| Hazard | Potential Consequence | Required Control Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Suspended loads | Crushing injuries, dropped objects, or equipment damage | Use an approved lifting plan, certified lifting equipment, and designated exclusion zones |
| Trapped pressure | Uncontrolled release of fluids or energy | Verify isolation and zero-energy status using approved site procedures |
| Hot work | Fire, burns, or ignition of flammable materials | Apply the approved hot-work procedure and required gas-testing controls |
| Stored hydraulic energy | Injection injuries or uncontrolled equipment movement | Follow the equipment manufacturer's isolation and depressurization instructions |
| Manual handling | Strains, impact injuries, or hand injuries | Use suitable handling aids and task-specific controls |
| Incorrect equipment configuration | Seal failure, connection damage, or loss of containment | Verify equipment identification, ratings, compatibility, and approved installation documentation |

### 3.2 Personal Protective Equipment

## 5. Installation Workflow

The following stages provide a documentation framework. The detailed execution steps and mandatory hold points must come from the approved installation procedure for the specific wellhead assembly.

### Step 1: Preparation and Casing Verification

**Objective:** Confirm that the installation can proceed using the correct equipment, approved references, and verified casing information.

Documentation and verification requirements:

1. Confirm the approved well program, work pack, and applicable risk assessments are available.
2. Verify the equipment model, serial number, pressure rating, connection details, and compatibility with the casing program.
3. Confirm the required well-status and isolation checks have been completed by authorized personnel.
4. Verify casing dimensions, condition, reference measurements, and preparation requirements against the approved drawings.
5. Record the inspection results and resolve discrepancies before proceeding.

**Hold point:** Installation must not proceed until required well-status checks, equipment identification, and casing acceptance criteria have been verified and authorized.

### Step 2: Equipment Inspection and Seal Verification

**Objective:** Confirm that the equipment and sealing components are correct and suitable for the specified assembly.

Inspection checklist:

- [ ] Equipment identification and serial numbers recorded
- [ ] Pressure ratings and connection specifications verified
- [ ] Relevant sealing surfaces inspected against manufacturer acceptance criteria
- [ ] Seals and sealing components checked against the approved parts list
- [ ] Components inspected for visible damage, corrosion, or contamination
- [ ] Required lubricants and assembly materials verified against manufacturer instructions
- [ ] Inspection findings recorded and reviewed

Damaged, unidentified, incompatible, or nonconforming components must be referred for technical disposition in accordance with the approved quality process.

### Step 3: Lifting, Positioning, and Alignment

**Objective:** Document the controlled positioning of the wellhead assembly.

1. Verify that the approved lifting plan and equipment requirements have been satisfied.
2. Confirm that the handling tools and lifting points are suitable for the identified assembly.
3. Record the equipment orientation and alignment requirements specified in the approved drawings.
4. Verify the assembly position and alignment using the manufacturer's specified acceptance criteria and suitable measurement methods.
5. Record inspection results and resolve any discrepancy before the next authorized stage.

**Hold point:** Do not accept the assembly based solely on visual alignment or a spirit-level reading. Use the acceptance criteria specified for the actual equipment configuration.

### Step 4: Connection Installation and Verification

**Objective:** Confirm that the specified connection is installed in accordance with the applicable manufacturer's instructions.

The connection method must be established from the approved equipment configuration. Welding, slip-on, set-screw, and other connection arrangements are not interchangeable unless the manufacturer and approved engineering documentation explicitly permit the selected configuration.

Verification requirements:

- Confirm the specified connection type and applicable drawings.
- Verify that the required joining procedure and qualified personnel are available.
- Confirm that applicable materials, consumables, and tools meet the approved requirements.
- Record required inspection, dimensional verification, and NDT results, where applicable.
- Verify completion of the specified acceptance checks before proceeding.

**Important:** Welding parameters, preheat requirements, torque values, tightening sequences, and other connection-specific instructions must be taken from the applicable approved procedure. They must not be inferred from this portfolio sample.

### Step 5: Pressure Testing and Acceptance

**Objective:** Document the approved test requirements, execution records, and acceptance decision for the installed assembly.

The test method, test medium, pressure stages, hold times, boundaries, and acceptance criteria depend on the specific equipment, connection, applicable standards, and approved test procedure.

Before testing, verify that:

- The approved test procedure and equipment limits are available.
- The test boundary and components included in the test are clearly identified.
- Required instruments are suitable and within calibration.
- Isolation, exclusion zones, communication, and pressure-release controls are established.
- The test is authorized and supervised by competent personnel.

Record the following information:

| Test Record Field | Required Information |
|---|---|
| Equipment identification | Equipment description, model, and serial number |
| Approved test procedure | Document number and revision |
| Test boundary | Components and connections included |
| Test medium | As specified in the approved procedure |
| Test stages | Approved pressure values and hold times |
| Instrumentation | Instrument IDs and calibration details |
| Observations | Recorded pressure, duration, leakage observations, and relevant findings |
| Acceptance criteria | Criteria defined by the approved procedure |
| Final result | Pass, fail, or pending technical review |
| Authorization | Names, roles, signatures, and date of authorized reviewers |

**Acceptance rule:** Do not use the pressure values or hold times in the original draft as approved test requirements. Confirm all values and acceptance criteria against the applicable manufacturer documentation and approved test procedure. Any failure must be managed through the approved depressurization, investigation, corrective-action, and retesting process.

## 6. Contingency and Troubleshooting

The table below provides a documentation framework for recording discrepancies. Corrective actions must be approved for the actual equipment and operating conditions.

| Issue | Possible Cause | Required Response |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure-test failure | Seal damage, connection defect, equipment malfunction, or test-system issue | Stop the test in accordance with the approved procedure; secure the system and investigate before corrective work |
| Alignment outside acceptance criteria | Dimensional discrepancy, casing preparation issue, or positioning error | Suspend progression and request engineering review against the approved drawings and tolerances |
| Connection or fastener discrepancy | Incorrect components, damaged threads, or assembly outside specification | Stop and refer to the manufacturer's instructions and approved corrective-action process |
| Seal damage or contamination | Improper handling, incompatible materials, or surface defects | Quarantine the affected components and obtain an approved inspection and disposition |
| Missing equipment traceability | Incomplete identification or documentation | Resolve the traceability gap before equipment acceptance |
| Incomplete test records | Missing readings, calibration details, or authorization | Hold final acceptance until the required records are completed and reviewed |

All discrepancies should be recorded with a unique reference, description, responsible person, corrective action, and closure evidence.
