    Wellhead Installation Procedure (API 10K Multi-Bowl)

    1. Document Control

    • Document ID: SOP-OPS-WH-10K-001
    • Version: 2.0
    • Effective Date: October 2026
    • Manufacturer Spec: Cameron Specialty Multi-Bowl System (MN-S)
    • Author: Drilling & Completions Engineering Team

    2. Objective & Scope

    This procedure outlines the technical and safety steps required to safely land, align, test, and secure a surface wellhead assembly. This applies to API 10,000 psi rated multi-bowl systems that housing both the surface casing hanger and intermediate casing strings within a single-piece compact body to minimize BOP removal steps.

    3. HSE Requirements & Hazards

    ⚠️ CRITICAL SAFETY NOTE: All operations must be performed under an active Permit to Work (PTW). Ensure Gas Testing is completed before any hot work or welding begins.

    Key Hazards

    • Suspended Loads: High risk during the hoisting and landing of heavy compact spools.
    • Trapped Pressure: Risk of trapped pressure below old wellhead sections or temporary caps.
    • Hot Work: Sparks and flames during casing cutting and wellhead welding.

    Required PPE

    • Standard site PPE (hard hat, steel-toe boots, safety glasses, coveralls).
    • High-impact gloves for handling tools and heavy equipment.
    • Welding hood and leather jacket (if a welded connection is chosen).

    4. Required Tools & Equipment

    • [ ] Cameron API 6A 10,000 psi Compact Multi-Bowl Wellhead Housing
    • [ ] High-capacity hydraulic casing cutter (or mechanical cutting saw)
    • [ ] Hydraulic flange bolt torque wrench (calibrated up to 5,000 ft-lbs)
    • [ ] Manufacturer-specific running tool / landing joint / lifting eyes
    • [ ] High-pressure test pump and digital chart recorder
    • [ ] Non-destructive testing (NDT) dye penetrant kit
    • [ ] Original Cameron elastomeric / metal-to-metal "P-seals"

    5. Step-by-Step Installation Procedure

    Step 1: Preparation & Casing Cut-Off

    1. Isolate the well: Ensure the well is completely dead, stable, and monitored for pressure or fluid influx.
    2. Expose the casing: Clean the surface casing string of cement, scale, and debris.
    3. Measure and mark: Mark the exact cut-off height on the conductor or surface casing based on the rig floor reference level.
    4. Execute the cut: Perform the rough and final cuts using a hydraulic casing cutter to ensure a smooth, perfectly horizontal face.
    5. Bevel the casing: Bevel the outer and inner edges of the casing to protect the wellhead internal seals from damage during landing.

    Step 2: Inspection & Seal Preparation

    • Inspect the casing head ring grooves for any scratches or pitting.
    • Thoroughly clean the internal elastomeric seals and lubricate them with an approved manufacturer-specified grease.
    • Verify the casing outside diameter (OD) matches the wellhead slip-on/welding bore specification.

    Step 3: Landing the Casing Head (A-Section)

    1. Attach approved lifting eyes and slings to the casing head.
    2. Slowly hoist the housing using the rig floor winch or crane.
    3. Lower the casing head smoothly over the casing stub.
      • Note: Ensure the side outlets align with the planned flowline or choke manifold orientation.
    4. Verify that the housing sits completely level using a precision spirit level.

    Step 4: Securing the Connection (Welded or Slip-On)

    Depending on the specific wellhead design, execute one of the following methods:

    Option A: Welded Type

    • Preheat the casing and wellhead base according to welding procedures.
    • Apply the structural root pass and filler passes.
    • Allow the weld to cool slowly. Do not quench with water.

    Option B: Slip-on / Set-Screw Type

    • Tighten the external set-screws crosswise to the manufacturer's recommended torque values.

    Step 5: Pressure Testing

    1. Connect the hydraulic test pump to the wellhead test port.
    2. Energize the internal seals by pumping clean water or hydraulic fluid.
    3. Hold a Low Pressure test at 250 psi for 5 minutes.
    4. Ramp up to the High Pressure test (10,000 psi) for 15 minutes.
    5. Record the results on a digital chart recorder; verify there is zero pressure drop.

    6. Contingency & Troubleshooting

    Issue Potential Cause Remedial Action
    Pressure test failure Damaged O-ring or seal Depressurize system, change out seal, re-clean, and re-test.
    Wellhead not level Uneven casing cut-off Lift wellhead, re-grind the casing stub level, and re-seat.
    Flange bolt misalignment Rotational misalignment Use aligning pins before setting the full weight of the spool.
